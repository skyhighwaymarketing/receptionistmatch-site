## Which trial fits you

**Upfirst** is the lowest-risk test. No card, no auto-billing, and 100 calls is about 7 calls a day across 14 days. There's also a free 30-minute onboarding call.
**Not for you if** your phone rings more than about 7 times a day. You'll hit the 100-call cap before the two weeks are up.

**Smith.ai's free plan** suits a low-volume office that may never need to pay. 25 calls a month covers light after-hours traffic. Hard calls can go to a Smith.ai live receptionist.
**Not for you if** you get more than 25 calls a month. Extra calls cost $3.00 each on the free plan, and canceling needs 30 days' notice.

**Goodcall's demo agent** lets you build and hear the receptionist before paying anything.
**Not for you if** you want to see how it handles real customers. Nothing answers your actual line until you pay.

**Rosie, Dialzara and My AI Front Desk** give 7 days. That's enough for one full week of calls, which is the test below.
**Not for you if** you can't spend an hour setting it up on day one. A slow start eats the trial.

**GoHighLevel** bills automatically when the 14 days end, and usage fees can apply during the trial.
**Not for you if** you just want calls answered. It's a full marketing platform; see [what an AI receptionist really costs](/ai-receptionist-cost/).

## How to test an AI receptionist in one week

Seven days is enough if you plan it.

1. **Day 1: pull your missed-call log.** Count last week's missed calls and note when they happened. That's your baseline and tells you if the trial cap is big enough.
2. **Day 1: forward only what you miss.** Send after-hours and unanswered calls to the trial number. Upfirst lets you choose every call, overflow only, or after hours. To compare which services cover nights and holidays and what each does on an emergency call, see our [after-hours answering service guide](/after-hours-answering-service/). Keep answering what you can.
3. **Day 2: call it yourself five times.** Ask your real questions: price for a common job, your service area, Saturday hours, a booking. Write down every wrong answer and fix it.
4. **Day 3: run an after-hours test.** Call at 10 p.m. from a number it doesn't know. Did the message reach you, and how fast?
5. **Day 4: test the hard call.** Pretend it's an emergency, like a burst pipe or a tooth that won't stop bleeding. Check whether it transfers, takes a message, or makes something up. HVAC owners can see which plans reach an on-call tech or a live person in our [HVAC answering service guide](/hvac-answering-service/). Transfers are missing from some cheap plans; see [which services hand calls to a person](/ai-phone-answering-service/).
6. **Days 5 to 6: read every summary.** Compare the call log against your baseline. Count callbacks you'd have lost.
7. **Day 7: decide before it bills.** Put a cancel reminder in your phone on day 1. Check which unit the paid plan counts: calls, minutes or unique callers.

## Free trials from answering services

If the test shows your callers need a person, live answering services offer trials too:

- **[PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/):** 14 days free on any plan. When it ends, service continues and your plan begins. From $99 for 50 minutes.
- **[Posh](https://www.posh.com/pricing):** 7 days or 250 minutes, whichever comes first, no card. From $130 for 50 minutes.
- **[Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/pricing/):** a free trial on every plan, no setup fee. From $164 for 50 Abby Minutes.
- **[Smith.ai live](https://smith.ai/pricing):** no trial, but a 30-day money-back guarantee, up to $1,000, overage excluded. From $300 for 30 calls.
- **[Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/):** no trial mentioned. From $250 for 50 minutes.
