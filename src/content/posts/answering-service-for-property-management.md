Prices come from each vendor's own pricing page, checked November 22, 2026.

An AI receptionist is software that answers your phone. An answering service is live people. Property managers get four kinds of calls: after-hours maintenance, real emergencies, tenants who speak Spanish, and leasing or showing calls. Pick the tool that handles the calls you actually get.

## After-hours maintenance and emergencies

Some night calls are a dripping faucet. Some are a burst pipe. What you're paying for is the sorting.

- **Upfirst** follows your list. You say what's urgent, like a burst pipe or a security issue, and what can wait. It sends a "flash notification" on the urgent ones and logs the rest for your morning review. It can collect unit numbers and access instructions. Waterfall transfers try one person after another, on every plan.
- **My AI Front Desk** says it takes the request, triages it, creates the work order, and escalates true emergencies to your on-call staff with the full transcript. The $99 plan lists "2 of each" agent action, such as a call transfer, and 3 notification recipients. Check that this covers your on-call rotation.
- **AnswerHero** puts a person on the line 24/7/365. It lists no holiday fees, no setup fees and no contracts, plus call dispatching and live message relay on every plan.
- **Smith.ai** texts or Slacks you so you can accept or reject a transfer: $0.50 a call, plus $0.25 for each extra person it tries.

**Not for you if** nobody on your on-call list answers at 2 a.m. A transfer only works if someone picks up. For nights-only pricing, see our [after-hours answering service guide](/after-hours-answering-service/).

## Tenants who call in Spanish

- **AnswerHero** lists "100% Bilingual" on every plan, including the $39 Economy plan.
- **Upfirst** detects the caller's language, answers in English, Spanish or 30+ others, and translates the transcript into yours. Its property page says it can also transfer Spanish callers straight to a bilingual human.
- **My AI Front Desk** lists 20+ languages. Whether transcripts are translated, the pricing page doesn't say.
- **Ruby** lists Spanish and bilingual handling as available. Confirm it's in your plan before you sign.
- **Smith.ai** charges $1.00 per call for a dedicated Spanish line. On the $300 plan's 30 calls, that's about $30 more.

More detail is in our [bilingual answering service guide](/bilingual-answering-service/).

## Leasing and showing calls

Upfirst books tours on Google, Outlook, Acuity or Calendly and asks your qualifying questions: move-in date, budget, pets. My AI Front Desk books tours and, on Entrata, reads tour scheduling and application status. Smith.ai qualifies leads at no charge and books appointments for $1.50 a call. Ruby lists scheduling and lead qualification. AnswerHero lists lead capture.

## Which tools touch your property software

**My AI Front Desk** names the most systems. Yardi: leads, guest cards and work orders sync both ways with Voyager and RentCafe. Rent Manager: work orders open, update and close from a resident's call. AppFolio: maintenance requests stay current. The catch: its pricing page lists Zapier on the $99 plan and puts "API access & custom integrations" on the custom-priced Partner/Enterprise plan. The pricing page doesn't say which plan includes the Yardi sync. Get the answer in writing.

**Upfirst** connects through Zapier and API integrations on every plan. Its property page says managers use this to create maintenance tickets. Done-for-you integrations start on the $299 Scale plan.

**AnswerHero, Ruby and Smith.ai** don't name any property management software on their pricing pages. Smith.ai can send call details to your CRM or Zapier. Ruby mentions 5000+ integrations but names no property software. Ask whether a message can reach your system, or plan on someone keying it in.

## Who each one is for

**AnswerHero** fits a manager who wants a person sorting emergencies, in English or Spanish, at the lowest published live price. **Not for you if** you want work orders created in your software without retyping.

**Upfirst** fits small and mid-size portfolios that want cheap 24/7 triage, tour booking and translated notes. **Not for you if** you need a named Yardi connection without building it in Zapier.

**My AI Front Desk** fits managers on Yardi, AppFolio or Rent Manager who want calls to land as work orders and guest cards. **Not for you if** you want a human deciding what counts as an emergency.

**Smith.ai and Ruby** fit offices that want a person on leasing calls and accept the price. **Not for you if** most calls are routine maintenance requests. You'd pay live rates for message-taking.

## How to pick

1. **Pull a month of calls.** Split them into maintenance, emergencies, Spanish and leasing.
2. **Write your emergency list.** Burst pipe, no heat, lockout. Every vendor here works from your rules.
3. **Ask about your software.** Which plan includes it, and does it write back or just send an email?
4. **Test it at night.** Upfirst gives 14 days or 100 calls with no card. My AI Front Desk gives 7 days. See every [AI receptionist free trial](/ai-receptionist-free-trial/), and compare [AI receptionist vs answering service](/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service/).
