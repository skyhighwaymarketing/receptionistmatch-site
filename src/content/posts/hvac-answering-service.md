## July math: what a spike costs by billing unit

Here is a worked example, not a statistic. Say your phone takes 80 calls in a mild month and 200 in July. Calls run about 2.5 minutes, which is PATLive's own estimate (50 minutes is "about 20 calls"). In July, people call back to ask where the tech is, so assume 150 different callers.

| Service | 80 calls (200 min) | 200 calls (500 min) | How it's covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Upfirst](https://www.upfirst.ai/pricing) | $59.95 | $159.95 | Premium (90 calls), then Pro (300 calls) |
| [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing) | $79 | about $118.50 | Starter, plus 50 extra callers at 79¢ |
| [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/pricing) | $149 | $149 | Scale, needed for transfers; 1,000 minutes covers both |
| [Smith.ai AI](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) | about $162.50 | about $385 | 75 calls plus extras at $2.50; 150 calls plus extras at $2.30 |
| [VoiceNation AI](https://www.voicenation.com/pricing/) | about $170.70 | about $418 | 50 calls plus extras at $2.39; 100 calls plus extras at $2.19 |
| [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) | $349 | $759 | 200-minute plan, then 500-minute plan |
| [VoiceNation live](https://www.voicenation.com/pricing/) | $422 | $985 | 200-minute plan, then 500-minute plan |

What this shows:

- **Per unique caller** handles callbacks best. On Goodcall, a homeowner who calls five times in July counts once.
- **Per minute** punishes long calls. A panicked no-cool call runs long, and live minutes cost dollars, not cents.
- **Per call** sits in between. Upfirst doesn't bill spam or calls under 15 seconds.

Change plans before the heat hits. PATLive and Upfirst let you upgrade or downgrade anytime. Smith.ai bills a new plan from the next month. VoiceNation needs 30 days' notice to cancel.

## If you already run Jobber or Housecall Pro

**Jobber Receptionist** answers calls and texts 24/7 and books jobs into your Jobber calendar. It's a $29 add-on on a Jobber plan (Core is $49 a month with no commitment) and included in Plus at $499 a month. The pricing page doesn't say how many calls it covers or whether it can hand a caller to a person.

**Housecall Pro CSR AI** "answers calls and books jobs 24/7." The pricing page doesn't show its price. Plans start at $79 a month for Basic, with a 14-day trial and no card. Book a demo to get a number.

**Not for you if** you need a guaranteed path to a human on emergency calls. Neither page says how that works. Ask before you rely on it.

## Standalone AI receptionists

**Upfirst** is the cheapest per-call option, and waterfall transfers on every plan try one person after another until someone answers. That fits an on-call rotation. You can set it to answer every call, overflow only, or after hours.

**Goodcall** suits a busy summer with lots of repeat callers.

**Rosie** needs the $149 Scale plan for transfers. Waterfall transfers start at $299.

**Smith.ai's AI Receptionist** costs more, but a live Smith.ai receptionist can take the call when the AI can't.

**Not for you if** nobody answers a transferred call at night. A transfer to a sleeping tech is voicemail with extra steps.

## Live answering service for no-heat, no-cool calls

**PATLive** has the lowest live prices here. Every plan includes emergency dispatch and 24/7 answering with no night or weekend fees, and you pay only for time a receptionist is on your call. It doesn't use AI at all. It can also take only the calls you don't answer, through ring-no-answer forwarding. 14-day free trial.

**VoiceNation** sells live plans from $165 for 50 minutes, with warm transfers included. It also sells AI plans, so you can mix the two.

Continental Message Solution wasn't part of this price check. See [answering service cost](/answering-service-cost/) for its plans.

**Not for you if** most of your calls are tune-up bookings and filter questions. You'd pay live rates for answers software gives at a fraction of the cost.

## Who needs a live person for emergencies

You need a person on the line if:

- Your callers include older homeowners or families with no heat, and you want a calm human voice.
- Someone has to decide whether a call is worth an after-hours truck roll.
- Your on-call tech doesn't reliably pick up transfers.

If your on-call phone always gets answered, an AI receptionist with transfers is enough. A middle path: AI during the day, live service after hours. For more on that split, read [AI receptionist vs answering service](/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service/) and [AI phone answering services with live backup](/ai-phone-answering-service/).

## How to pick

1. **Pull last July's call log.** Count calls, minutes and repeat callers. That tells you which billing unit is cheapest.
2. **Count the after-hours emergencies.** If there are only a few, a live plan for nights only may be enough.
3. **Test it in the shoulder season.** Use a trial in spring, not mid-heatwave. Here's every [AI receptionist free trial](/ai-receptionist-free-trial/).
4. **Size up in June, down in September.** Check each vendor's plan-change terms first.
